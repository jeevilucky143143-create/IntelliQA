Quantum computing is a rapidly emerging area of computer science that harnesses the fundamental laws of quantum mechanics to solve complex problems faster than classical supercomputers.

Unlike classical computers which process information using binary bits representing 0 or 1, quantum computers utilize quantum bits or qubits. Qubits leverage quantum phenomena such as superposition and entanglement to represent multiple combinations of states simultaneously.

Superposition allows qubits to exist in a linear combination of states, enabling quantum algorithms to evaluate exponential possibilities at once. Entanglement links the state of one qubit to another, creating instantaneous correlations that enhance processing speed and computational throughput.

Prominent quantum programming frameworks include IBM Qiskit, Google Cirq, and Microsoft Q#. IBM Quantum provides cloud-based access to physical superconducting quantum processors for researchers worldwide.

Key applications of quantum computing include quantum cryptography, molecular chemistry simulation for drug discovery, complex financial modeling, supply chain optimization, and accelerating machine learning algorithms.
